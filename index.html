01 · The big picture

What is a tense?

A tense helps us show when an action happens and sometimes how it develops over time.

PASTActions before now
PRESENTNow, habits and experience
FUTUREPredictions and plans
The traditional learning framework presents 12 core tenses, plus the near-future structure “be going to”.
02
02 · How to read formulas

Meet the building blocks

S = Subject

The person or thing doing the action: I, you, she, they.

V1 = Base verb

go, study, write. The dictionary form.

V2 = Past form

went, studied, wrote.

V3 = Past participle

gone, studied, written.

O = Object

The person or thing affected by the action.

V-ing

Verb + -ing: studying, playing, reading.

03
03 · Present time · 1 of 4

Present Simple

Habits, routines, facts and things that are generally true.

FORMULA
(+) S + V1(s/es) · (−) S + do/does not + V1
(?) Do/Does + S + V1?
WHEN TO USE IT
  • Daily routines and repeated actions
  • Facts and general truths
  • Permanent situations
SIGNAL WORDS

always · usually · often · sometimes · never · every day

EXAMPLE

She studies English every evening.

She has a regular study habit.

04
04 · Present time · 2 of 4

Present Continuous

An action happening now or around the present period.

FORMULA
(+) S + am/is/are + V-ing
(−) S + am/is/are not + V-ing
(?) Am/Is/Are + S + V-ing?
WHEN TO USE IT
  • Actions happening at the moment
  • Temporary situations
  • Arrangements in the near future
SIGNAL WORDS

now · right now · at the moment · currently

EXAMPLE

I am preparing my presentation now.

The action is in progress.

05
05 · Present time · 3 of 4

Present Perfect

Past actions connected to the present: experiences, results or situations continuing until now.

FORMULA
(+) S + have/has + V3
(−) S + have/has not + V3
(?) Have/Has + S + V3?
WHEN TO USE IT
  • Life experiences
  • Recent actions with present results
  • Actions continuing from past to now
SIGNAL WORDS

already · yet · just · ever · never · since · for

EXAMPLE

We have finished our project.

The result matters now.

06
06 · Present time · 4 of 4

Present Perfect Continuous

An action that started in the past and has continued up to now, with emphasis on duration.

FORMULA
(+) S + have/has been + V-ing
(−) S + have/has not been + V-ing
(?) Have/Has + S + been + V-ing?
WHEN TO USE IT
  • Emphasising how long an action has continued
  • Recent ongoing activity with visible results
SIGNAL WORDS

for · since · all day · lately · recently · how long

EXAMPLE

She has been learning English for three years.

The learning began in the past and continues.

07
07 · Past time · 1 of 4

Past Simple

Completed actions at a finished time in the past.

FORMULA
(+) S + V2/ed
(−) S + did not + V1
(?) Did + S + V1?
WHEN TO USE IT
  • Finished past actions
  • A sequence of past events
  • Past habits or states
SIGNAL WORDS

yesterday · last week · ago · in 2020

EXAMPLE

They visited Da Lat last summer.

The visit is finished.

08
08 · Past time · 2 of 4

Past Continuous

An action in progress at a particular time in the past.

FORMULA
(+) S + was/were + V-ing
(−) S + was/were not + V-ing
(?) Was/Were + S + V-ing?
WHEN TO USE IT
  • An action in progress at a past time
  • A longer action interrupted by a shorter one
  • Two actions happening simultaneously
SIGNAL WORDS

while · when · as · at 8 p.m. yesterday

EXAMPLE

I was reading when my friend called.

Reading was in progress; the call interrupted it.

09
09 · Past time · 3 of 4

Past Perfect

An action completed before another point or action in the past.

FORMULA
(+) S + had + V3
(−) S + had not + V3
(?) Had + S + V3?
WHEN TO USE IT
  • The earlier of two past actions
  • Something completed before a past deadline
SIGNAL WORDS

before · after · by the time · already · until then

EXAMPLE

The train had left before we arrived.

First: the train left. Later: we arrived.

10
10 · Past time · 4 of 4

Past Perfect Continuous

An ongoing action that continued for a period before another past event.

FORMULA
(+) S + had been + V-ing
(−) S + had not been + V-ing
(?) Had + S + been + V-ing?
WHEN TO USE IT
  • Emphasising duration before a past moment
  • Explaining a past result or condition
SIGNAL WORDS

for · since · before · until · by the time

EXAMPLE

He had been studying for hours before the test.

The study period came before the test.

11
11 · Future time · 1 of 4

Future Simple

Predictions, promises, offers and decisions made at the moment of speaking.

FORMULA
(+) S + will + V1
(−) S + will not + V1
(?) Will + S + V1?
WHEN TO USE IT
  • Predictions and opinions
  • Spontaneous decisions
  • Promises and offers
SIGNAL WORDS

tomorrow · next week · soon · probably · I think

EXAMPLE

I think our team will win.

A prediction about the future.

12
12 · Future time · 2 of 4

Future Continuous

An action that will be in progress at a specific time in the future.

FORMULA
(+) S + will be + V-ing
(−) S + will not be + V-ing
(?) Will + S + be + V-ing?
WHEN TO USE IT
  • Actions in progress at a future time
  • Expected events as part of a routine
SIGNAL WORDS

this time tomorrow · at 8 p.m. · next week

EXAMPLE

At 9 tomorrow, I will be taking my exam.

The exam will be in progress at 9.

13
13 · Future time · 3 of 4

Future Perfect

An action that will be completed before a specific future time.

FORMULA
(+) S + will have + V3
(−) S + will not have + V3
(?) Will + S + have + V3?
WHEN TO USE IT
  • Completion before a future deadline
  • Looking back from a future point
SIGNAL WORDS

by · by the time · before · by next year

EXAMPLE

By Friday, we will have finished the project.

The project will be complete before Friday ends.

14
14 · Future time · 4 of 4

Future Perfect Continuous

An action continuing over a period up to a particular point in the future.

FORMULA
(+) S + will have been + V-ing
(−) S + will not have been + V-ing
(?) Will + S + have been + V-ing?
WHEN TO USE IT
  • Emphasising duration up to a future moment
  • Highlighting how long an activity will have continued
SIGNAL WORDS

for · by · by the time · by next month

EXAMPLE

By June, she will have been working here for five years.

The duration reaches five years in June.

15 · Extra structure

Near Future: Be Going To

Often taught alongside the 12 tenses. It is used for plans and predictions based on present evidence.

FORMULA
(+) S + am/is/are + going to + V1
(−) S + am/is/are not + going to + V1
(?) Am/Is/Are + S + going to + V1?

Plans and intentions

We are going to visit Hue this summer.

Evidence-based prediction

Look at those clouds! It is going to rain.

Quick contrast: “will” often expresses a spontaneous decision or general prediction; “be going to” often expresses an existing plan or prediction based on evidence.

16 · Compare and connect

One verb, different timelines

Notice how the verb “study” changes the meaning when the time structure changes.

Present Simple

I study English every day.

Present Continuous

I am studying English now.

Past Simple

I studied English yesterday.

Future Simple

I will study English tomorrow.

Tip: First identify the time reference. Then decide whether the sentence focuses on a routine, an action in progress, a completed result or duration.
