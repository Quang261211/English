01 · Big picture

What does a tense tell us?

A tense places an action in time. The form also helps show whether we see it as a fact, an action in progress, a completed result, or an activity continuing over a period.

PASTBefore the moment of speaking
PRESENTNow, habits and experience
FUTUREPlans, predictions and later events
Think about the time relationship first. Signal words are clues, not automatic answers.
02 · Formula toolkit

Know your building blocks

S

Subject: I, you, he, she, they.

V1

Base verb: go, study, write.

V2

Past form: went, studied, wrote.

V3

Past participle: gone, studied, written.

V-ing

Verb + ing: going, studying, writing.

O

Object: the person or thing affected.

In negatives and questions, auxiliaries such as do, be, have and will carry the tense information.
03 · The four patterns

Four ways to view an action

SIMPLE

Facts, habits, or events viewed as a whole.

CONTINUOUS

An action in progress around a reference time.

PERFECT

A completed action connected to a later reference point.

PERFECT CONTINUOUS

Duration or ongoing activity leading up to a reference point.

Present Simple

Habits, routines, facts and general truths.

CORE FORMULA · AFFIRMATIVE

(−) Add the appropriate auxiliary + not
(?) Move the first auxiliary before the subject
COMMON TIME EXPRESSIONS

always · usually · often · every day

KEY POINT

Remember: he/she/it takes -s or -es in affirmative statements.

EXAMPLE

Past experiences, recent results and situations continuing until now.

(−) Add the appropriate auxiliary + not
(?) Move the first auxiliary before the subject
COMMON TIME EXPRESSIONS

KEY POINT

Past Simple

CORE FORMULA · AFFIRMATIVE

(−) Add the appropriate auxiliary + not
(?) Move the first auxiliary before the subject
COMMON TIME EXPRESSIONS

KEY POINT

09 · Past time · 6 of 13

Past Continuous

An action in progress at a particular past moment, often around another event.

CORE FORMULA · AFFIRMATIVE
S + was/were + V-ing
(−) Add the appropriate auxiliary + not
(?) Move the first auxiliary before the subject
COMMON TIME EXPRESSIONS

while · when · as · at 8 p.m. yesterday

KEY POINT

The longer background action often uses Past Continuous; the interrupting event often uses Past Simple.

EXAMPLE

12 · Future time · 9 of 13

Future Simple

Predictions, promises, offers and decisions made at the moment of speaking.

CORE FORMULA · AFFIRMATIVE
S + will + V1
(−) Add the appropriate auxiliary + not
(?) Move the first auxiliary before the subject
COMMON TIME EXPRESSIONS

tomorrow · soon · probably · I think

KEY POINT

Will is followed by the base verb, without to.

EXAMPLE

13 · Future time · 10 of 13

Future Continuous

An action that will be in progress at a specific future time.

CORE FORMULA · AFFIRMATIVE
S + will be + V-ing
(−) Add the appropriate auxiliary + not
(?) Move the first auxiliary before the subject
COMMON TIME EXPRESSIONS

this time tomorrow · at 8 p.m. · next week

KEY POINT

Imagine looking at that future moment while the action is underway.

EXAMPLE

14 · Future time · 11 of 13

Future Perfect

An action completed before a specific future deadline.

CORE FORMULA · AFFIRMATIVE

(−) Add the appropriate auxiliary + not
(?) Move the first auxiliary before the subject
COMMON TIME EXPRESSIONS

by · by the time · before · by next year

KEY POINT

By often introduces the deadline by which completion is expected.

EXAMPLE

15 · Future time · 12 of 13

Future Perfect Continuous

An activity continuing over a period up to a future reference point.

CORE FORMULA · AFFIRMATIVE
S + will have been + V-ing
(−) Add the appropriate auxiliary + not
(?) Move the first auxiliary before the subject
COMMON TIME EXPRESSIONS

for · by · by the time · by next month

KEY POINT

It focuses on how long the activity will have continued by that future point.

EXAMPLE

By June, she will have been working here for five years.

16 · Extra structure · 13 of 13

Near Future · Be Going To

Existing plans and predictions based on present evidence.

CORE FORMULA · AFFIRMATIVE
S + am/is/are + going to + V1
(−) Add the appropriate auxiliary + not
(?) Move the first auxiliary before the subject
COMMON TIME EXPRESSIONS

tonight · tomorrow · soon · look!

KEY POINT

Often taught alongside the 12 core tenses. It is a future construction, not an extra core tense.

EXAMPLE

Look at those clouds! It is going to rain.

Read the time reference and the speaker's intended meaning.

17 · Compare meanings

One verb, different timelines

The verb “study” changes form because each sentence gives a different time perspective.

Present Simple

I study English every day.

Present Continuous

I am studying English now.

Past Simple

I studied English yesterday.

Future Simple

I will study English tomorrow.

Ask: When does it happen? Is it a habit, in progress, completed, or continuing for a duration?
18 · Common mistakes

Small details, big difference

Finished past time

❌ I have seen him yesterday.
✓ I saw him yesterday.

After did / didn't

❌ Did she went?
✓ Did she go?

Third-person -s

❌ He play football.
✓ He plays football.

State verbs

Usually: I know the answer.
Not usually: I am knowing the answer.

In time clauses referring to the future, use a present form after when, before, after, until and as soon as: “I'll call you when I arrive.”
