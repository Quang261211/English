Easy comparisons

Some tenses look similar. Focus on what the speaker wants to emphasize.

Present Simple vs Present Continuous

Present Simple = habits, facts, routines.
Present Continuous = something happening now or temporarily.

“I study English every day.” / “I am studying English now.”

Past Simple vs Present Perfect

Past Simple = a finished past time, often stated.
Present Perfect = past action connected to now; the exact finished time is not the focus.

“I visited Hue in 2024.” / “I have visited Hue.”

Present Perfect vs Present Perfect Continuous

Present Perfect often emphasizes a result or experience. Present Perfect Continuous emphasizes the activity or its duration.

“I have read three chapters.” / “I have been reading for two hours.”

Will vs Be going to

Will often expresses a spontaneous decision, promise, or prediction. Be going to often expresses an existing plan or a prediction based on present evidence.

“I'll answer the phone.” / “Look at those clouds. It's going to rain.”

Study note: The 12-tense framework is a common teaching model; “be going to” is included here as the 13th, near-future structure for learning convenience. Signal words are clues, not automatic rules.
